# Corbie / After hours

A local, silent, 44-second cinematic stage for recording the real desktop Corbie.
All artwork is drawn in the browser. No downloads, accounts, fonts, or external services.

## Record with the real bird

1. Choose **Goodnight, Corbie** on the old copy, then open **Corbie-v0.100.exe**.
2. In **Nest → Behavior → Habitat sandbox**, choose **Open cinema**. This also enables his nervous system.
3. Close any old clearing/cinema tabs. They share one pixel input and would compete.
4. Use a normal browser window. Keep Corbie awake, unpaused, and out of quiet mode. Close the Nest after opening the cinema.
5. Choose portrait or landscape, then **Set walls · B**. The page switches to Clean view. Drag a box around the full cinema frame in the desktop overlay. **Escape** cancels the selection. The box needs room for his full body, not just his feet. Now drag him onto the branch. Set up your recorder and crop it to the stage, including the actual desktop bird. Browser-only capture may omit a separate desktop pet window; a display capture includes him.
6. Let his relocation comment finish, then click an empty part of the forest to focus the browser. Press **C** to roll: rewind and count down three seconds. The film loops by default. Add your own soundtrack in the edit.

**B** draws walls; **O** opens them; **C** rolls the camera; **H** toggles the clean view; **Escape** restores controls; **Space** plays/pauses; **R** rewinds; **1–6** jump to a chapter. Showing the controls, resizing/moving the browser, or changing frame shape opens the walls because the set moved. Press **B** to draw them again. The small film signature says **ON SET** while the desktop confirms the walls. Closing/hiding the page releases the walls after about two seconds; disconnecting the habitat clears them immediately.

With walls set, walking turns at the sides and flights stay inside. A final position check also catches flings and other movements. The boundary reserves space for body gestures and upward hops. Your normal wandering, perching and flight settings are not permanently changed. **Open walls** explicitly releases him. No automatic browser-position guesses or invisible desktop-wide route are used for the set.

## What he sees

The actual 720 × 1280 portrait canvas (1280 × 720 in landscape) is reduced to a 48 × 32 grayscale frame and sent at up to 10 frames/second through the existing authenticated loopback bridge. The same motion circuit and startle adapter drive the desktop bird. The moth and shiny object are deliberately brighter than the moon, forest, and stars. Titles, controls, and the desktop bird are outside the sampled canvas.

The film moves from quiet to curiosity, a shiny object, fast fly-bys, a wider sky, and stillness. His attention, familiarity and current activities affect his response; no spoken lines or guaranteed jumps are scripted. Rewind does **not** erase his memory. Sleeping, fullscreen, speech, recording notes, and other existing busy states continue to take priority. The page shows the live reason where available.

Opening **cinema.html** directly plays the visuals without the live connection or walls. Keep **cinema.js** beside it. Live input and walls require opening it from the Nest on v0.100 or newer. Nothing is sent when the page is hidden; leaving the tab also pauses the film. Closing the page lets the existing stale-input cutoff release his attention.
